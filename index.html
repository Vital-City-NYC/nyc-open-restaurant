What this does and doesn't cover

A first-time, single-location, sit-down restaurant in a space already zoned for eating and drinking

  • Timelines are typical ranges, not guarantees. A space that already has a commercial kitchen, a current Certificate of Occupancy and no major construction can open far faster; a ground-up build-out, a landmarked building or a contested liquor license can take a year or more.
  • You can legally begin operating 22 days after you submit your Food Service Establishment Permit application, even before the pre-permit inspection — but a Certificate of Occupancy must already be in place, and alcohol cannot be served until the State Liquor Authority acts.
  • Not every step applies to every restaurant. Outdoor dining, a liquor license, a Place of Assembly permit (75 or more people) and Landmarks review are conditional.
  • Fees listed are government application or permit fees only. They exclude rent, construction, professional fees (architect, expediter, attorney) and insurance, which dwarf the permit costs.

Methodology & sources

Nothing here is a black box — here is where every figure comes from

  • Steps and the 22-day rule, permit fee ($280, plus $25 for frozen desserts), Food Protection Course, pre-permit inspection, Certificate of Authority and insurance requirements: NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene and NYC Business — Opening a Restaurant and Food Service Establishment Permit.
  • Construction sequence, Alteration permits, Certificate of Occupancy, FDNY hood suppression and per-step week ranges: Park Bench Architects, Opening a Restaurant in NYC: Step-by-Step Guide, cross-checked against NYC Department of Buildings guidance.
  • Liquor license fee ($4,352 full on-premises in New York City), 6–10 month timeline, 500-foot rule and 30-day community board notice: NYS Liquor Authority Restaurant License Quick Reference and the SLA retail license fee chart.
  • Outdoor dining license ($1,050 per café type for four years; up to six-month review): NYC Department of Transportation, Dining Out NYC fees.
  • Confidence: Government fees and the statutory rules (22-day, 500-foot, 30-day) are high confidence. Week-by-week durations are estimates drawn from professional practitioners and will vary by project; treat them as planning guides, not commitments.
